# Bridging the Gap: From RPC Audits to GPU Tunneling

As a CTO, my day rarely follows a linear path. One moment I'm diving into the minutiae of JSON-RPC method parity for a mainnet upgrade, and the next, I'm architecting a low-cost GPU bridge for a demo. Today was a prime example of this duality—balancing the rigid precision required for infrastructure stability with the agile "hacking" needed to enable rapid prototyping.

Here is a summary of today's technical milestones and the insights gained from the process.

## 1. Infrastructure Rigor: The RPC Surface Audit
A significant portion of today was dedicated to the `ops-surface` audit for the m113 upgrade. When dealing with blockchain indexers, "mostly compatible" isn't enough; a single mismatched response in a rare RPC method can lead to silent data corruption or indexer crashes.

**The Problem:** We needed to ensure that the new node version (cand) produced identical results to the current production source (`api1`) across all methods actually used by the synchronizer.

**The Technical Decision:** Instead of relying on a generic test suite, I performed a "literal extraction" from the `synchronizer.jar` to identify the exact method list being called. I then conducted A/B testing on critical methods like `debug_traceTransaction` and `getRawTransactionByHash`.

**Insight:** 
The diff shows a successful match: `trace/rawTx/batch A/B all match`. Interestingly, I found a discrepancy in `effectiveGasPrice` for batch calls, which was traced back to a specific fix (#106). This highlights why **differential testing** is superior to unit testing in infrastructure—it reveals the "delta" between two living systems.

## 2. Agile Enablement: The Gemma-4 GPU Tunnel
On the AI front, the goal was to provide the `daepa` demo environment with access to our high-performance internal GPU servers (H100 NVL x2) without incurring the massive costs of AWS P5 instances.

**The Challenge:** The demo app resides in a private VPC with no NAT gateway or VPC endpoints, making traditional outbound connectivity to our office servers impossible.

**The Solution: SSH-over-SSM Reverse Tunneling.**
I implemented a reverse tunnel architecture:
`Office GPU Server` $\rightarrow$ `AWS SSM` $\rightarrow$ `t4g.micro Landing EC2` $\rightarrow$ `Demo App`.

By using `autossh` and a restricted SSH key (limited to port forwarding), I created a secure, self-healing bridge. 

**Cost Optimization Insight:**
I specifically chose a `t4g.micro` with a public IP for SSM outbound traffic. While a NAT Gateway is the "standard" enterprise approach, it costs ~$40/month. My workaround brings the cost down to **~$12/month** while maintaining a tight security posture (SSH port 22 closed, only port 18002 open to the VPC).

```text
[Demo App] --VPC Internal--> [daepa-llm-tunnel EC2 :18002]
                                      ↑ Reverse SSH Tunnel (SSM)
[Office GPU Server] vLLM gemma-4-31b-it (H100 NVL ×2)
```

## 3. Reflections on the "CTO Mindset"
Looking at today's commits—ranging from `dashboard.json` updates to complex network tunneling—it's clear that the role of a technical leader is about **context switching**. 

In the RPC audit, I acted as a **Quality Gatekeeper**, where the priority was zero risk. In the GPU tunnel project, I acted as an **Enabler**, where the priority was speed and cost-efficiency. 

At **CPLABS**, we strive for this balance: the discipline to ensure our core blockchain infrastructure is bulletproof, and the creativity to leverage our internal resources to push the boundaries of what our AI demos can do.

- **AI Infrastructure:** Gemma-4 (31B) successfully bridged to `daepa` VPC.
- **Cost Savings:** Avoided expensive AWS GPU instances by utilizing internal H100s via a smart tunnel.
