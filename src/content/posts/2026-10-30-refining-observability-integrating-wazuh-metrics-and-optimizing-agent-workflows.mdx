# Refining Observability: Integrating Wazuh Metrics and Optimizing Agent Workflows

As a CTO and engineer, I’ve learned that the difference between a "working" system and a "reliable" system lies in the details of the observability pipeline. Today's work was a deep dive into exactly that—refining the integration between Wazuh and Zabbix to ensure that security events aren't just captured, but are actionable and noise-free.

## Solving the "Noise" Problem in Security Monitoring

The primary focus today was the progression of the Wazuh-to-Zabbix pipeline. We moved through Stage 2 verification and into Stage 3 metric implementation. 

One of the most critical technical lessons of the day came from a mistake I made during the Stage 2 trigger setup. I had initially configured the trigger prototypes as `type=1` (multiple events). In a high-frequency monitoring environment, this is a recipe for disaster. Because the `nodata` timer was evaluating every 30 seconds, it generated a flood of new PROBLEM events.

**The Fix:** I pivoted the trigger prototypes (27991~27993) to `type=0` (single event). 

In my 20 years of engineering, I've seen this pattern repeatedly: the temptation to "capture everything" often leads to "alert fatigue." By switching to single-event triggers and utilizing tag-scoped maintenance, we ensure that the Ops team only sees what actually requires their attention, rather than a mirror of the system's internal polling heartbeat.

## Stage 3: From Events to Metrics

With Stage 2 stabilized, I implemented the Stage 3 metrics. The goal here was to move beyond simple alerts and into health telemetry. I configured a master item (`wazuh.metrics`) with several dependent items using JSONPath to extract specific data:
- Active/Disconnected/Never Connected agents
- Vulnerability status (`vuln_ok`)
- Reboot requirements

The technical challenge here is the dependency on Low-Level Discovery (LLD). As seen in the logs, the items were created, but they remained empty until a discovery cycle was triggered. I had to coordinate a manual discovery request to CPLABS to validate that the prototypes were correctly spawning actual items for each agent.

## Final Insights

Today's work reinforces a core philosophy: **Observability is an iterative process.** You cannot build a perfect monitoring system in one go. You deploy, you observe the noise, you suppress the irrelevant, and you refine the triggers.

Whether it's adjusting a Zabbix trigger type or streamlining how a GitHub token is read, the goal is always the same: reduce friction and increase signal.
