# Balancing Precision and Automation: Lessons from Content Engineering

As a CTO, I often find that the most interesting technical challenges aren't always found in the core architecture of a blockchain or an AI model, but in the **pipeline engineering** required to deliver high-quality, automated content at scale.

Today's work focused on the intersection of data management and generative AI, specifically within my `booksummary` and `dashboard` projects. While the commits might look like simple "updates" or "feature additions," they represent a rigorous process of refining a production pipeline.

## 1. Data-Driven Content Selection
In the `booksummary` project, I completed the production of a summary for **"Bukhakui" (박제가)**. The technical decision-making here isn't about the writing, but the **selection criteria**.

I use a data-driven approach to determine which books to process, tracking "Demand vs. Competition" ratios. In the diff for `data/candidates.md`, you can see a re-measurement of the demand:
```diff
- | **6** | **북학의** (박제가) | 680 | 1,246 (1회) | 5 · 책 1 | 0.55 | 실학 고전. 「북학론」 간섭
+ | ~~6~~ | ~~북학의 (박제가)~~ ✅ 11/14 `kCFOVdbq7Ug` | 680 → 600 | 1,246 → 1,247 (2회) | 5 · 책 1 | 0.55 | 실학 고전. 「북학론」 간섭
```
By adjusting the demand from 680 to 600 based on second-pass measurements, I ensure the content strategy remains grounded in actual search trends rather than optimistic first impressions.

## 2. The "Hallucination" Battle in Generative AI
The most challenging part of today's work was the visual generation using Flux. When automating historical content, the "AI aesthetic" often clashes with historical accuracy.

I encountered a significant issue with **textual artifacts** (gibberish Hanja/Chinese characters) and **anachronisms** (modern power poles, striped shirts, and even yachts appearing in 18th-century Joseon).

To solve this, I made a strategic decision in `scripts/gen_flux_bukhak.py` to move away from "scene-based" prompts to "element-based" prompts. Instead of prompting for a "market street" (which triggers the AI to generate fake signs), I shifted to:
> *"wide dirt road / brick city wall seen from outside / close-up of goods"*

This is a practical insight: **When AI fails to generate a specific element correctly, the most efficient engineering solution is to remove that element from the prompt entirely rather than trying to "fix" it with negative prompts.**

The result? A high discard rate (24 out of 88 images), but a final set of 64 high-fidelity images that maintain the professional standard I expect.

## 3. Maintaining the "Pulse" with Dashboards
Finally, I updated the `dashboard.json` for my personal site. In a high-velocity environment—similar to how we monitor system health at **CPLABS**—having a visual representation of activity across multiple repositories (`claude-agent`, `booksummary`, etc.) is crucial. It prevents "developer blindness" by making the velocity of various workstreams explicit.

## Final Thoughts
Whether I'm building a DID system or an automated book summary pipeline, the principle remains the same: **Trust the data, but verify the output.** 

Automation is a force multiplier, but without the "human-in-the-loop" to prune the AI's hallucinations (like a 18th-century scholar wearing a striped shirt), the quality degrades. The goal is not 100% automation, but **100% quality through automated pipelines.**
