# The Discipline of Visibility: Why I Automate My Engineering Dashboard

As a CTO and engineer with over two decades in the trenches—ranging from the early days of blockchain infrastructure to the current frontier of AI and DID—I’ve learned that the most dangerous state for a technical leader is "invisible progress." 

When you are managing complex architectures at **CPLABS** or contributing to open-source agents, it is easy to spend a week deep in a rabbit hole of debugging or architectural refactoring without a tangible "paper trail" of progress. That is why I maintain a personal engineering dashboard.

Today’s work was a routine but essential update to my dashboard data (`dashboard.json`), but the act of updating these metrics provides a moment of reflection on my current velocity and focus.

### The Technical Implementation: Data-Driven Reflection

The change today was a `chore` update to the `dashboard.json` file. While it looks like a simple JSON diff, this file serves as the backend for my personal activity tracker. It aggregates commit counts, recent messages, and activity heatmaps across multiple repositories.

Looking at the diff, we can see the shift in focus:

```json
- "name": "claude-agent",
- "last_msg": "config: switch permissions.defaultMode from bypassPermissions to default",
...
+ "name": "claude-agent",
+ "last_msg": "docs: record GitGuardian triage and refresh the resume note",
+ "commits_7d": 34,
+ "commits_30d": 60
```

The `claude-agent` repository shows a significant spike in activity, with `commits_7d` jumping to 34. This reflects a period of intense iteration. Interestingly, the most recent change wasn't a feature addition, but a "GitGuardian triage." 

### Engineering Insight: The Importance of Security Triage

The mention of **GitGuardian** in the commit history is a critical point. In the Web3 and AI space, the cost of a leaked API key or a hardcoded secret is catastrophic. Whether I am working on internal tools at **CPLABS** or personal agents, integrating automated secret scanning into the CI/CD pipeline is non-negotiable.

Many developers treat "documentation" or "triage" as low-priority tasks. However, from my experience, the difference between a senior engineer and a lead engineer is the willingness to spend time on the "boring" parts: cleaning up security alerts, updating CHANGELOGs, and ensuring the codebase is maintainable for the next person.

### The Philosophy of the "Activity Array"

In the `dashboard.json`, I track activity as an array of integers:
`"activity": [0, 0, 3, 9, 8, 14, 0]`

This is a simplified version of a GitHub contribution graph. By quantifying my daily output, I can identify patterns of burnout or stagnation. For instance, seeing a string of zeros in certain repositories (like `metadium_homepage` or `heritageradio`) isn't necessarily a bad thing—it indicates a conscious shift in priority toward AI agents and DID infrastructure.

### Final Thoughts

Engineering is not just about writing code; it is about managing energy and focus. By automating the tracking of my commits and reflecting on them daily, I ensure that my technical trajectory aligns with my professional goals.

Whether you are building the next generation of decentralized identity systems or optimizing a corporate homepage at **CPLABS**, remember: **If you don't measure your progress, you are just guessing.**

Stay curious, stay secure.
