# The Art of Quantifying Progress: Maintaining the Engineering Dashboard

As a CTO and engineer with over two decades of experience, I’ve learned that visibility is the cornerstone of productivity. Whether you are managing a large-scale infrastructure at **CPLABS** or maintaining a personal suite of projects, the ability to quantify "velocity" is what separates a chaotic workflow from a disciplined one.

Today’s work was a routine but essential update to my personal development dashboard (`jsong1230.github.io`). While a `chore: Update dashboard data` commit might seem trivial at first glance, it represents a larger philosophy I hold regarding engineering management: **The Feedback Loop.**

### The Technical Context: Data-Driven Self-Management

The change involves updating `src/data/dashboard.json`, which serves as the single source of truth for my activity tracking across various repositories. By updating the `generated_at` timestamp and the activity arrays, I am essentially performing a "heartbeat" check on my current projects.

Looking at the diff, we can see the shift in focus across my active repositories:

```json
"name": "claude-agent",
"last_msg": "log: briefick explorer share rebalanced within 3.5h cap",
"activity": [2, 52, 49, 19, 17, 40, 0],
"commits_7d": 183,
"commits_30d": 435
```

The `claude-agent` project is clearly in a high-intensity phase, with 183 commits in the last seven days. In contrast, my `booksummary` project—a personal passion project—shows a steady, slower cadence.

### Technical Decisions: Why a JSON-based Dashboard?

Many developers rely on GitHub's native contribution graph (the "green squares"). However, for a professional who manages multiple identities (work, open source, and personal research), a native graph is often too noisy or too restrictive. 

I chose a decoupled JSON data structure for several reasons:
1. **Portability:** By storing the data in a JSON file, I can easily feed this into different frontend components or even an AI agent to analyze my productivity trends.
2. **Custom Metrics:** I can track specific metrics—like `commits_7d` vs `commits_30d`—that provide a more nuanced view of momentum than a simple calendar view.
3. **Automation:** This process is designed to be automated. The "Auto-generate daily posts" message in the `jsong1230.github.io` repo indicates a pipeline that reduces the friction of documentation.

### Engineering Insights: The Psychology of the Commit

There is a psychological advantage to seeing these numbers. When I see `commits_7d: 183` for a specific project, it validates the "deep work" state I've been in. Conversely, seeing a `0` in the activity array for a secondary project acts as a trigger to re-evaluate whether that project should be archived or revived.

In my years leading technical teams at **CPLABS**, I always encouraged my engineers to keep their own "brag documents" or activity logs. It is incredibly difficult to recall exactly what technical hurdles you overcame six months ago during a performance review or a post-mortem. By maintaining a data-driven log, you transform subjective memory into objective history.

### Closing Thoughts

Today's update is a reminder that the "boring" parts of development—maintenance, data updates, and logging—are what sustain the "exciting" parts. Whether it's blockchain architecture, DID implementation, or a simple JSON update, the discipline of tracking progress is what allows an engineer to scale their impact.

Stay disciplined, keep logging, and let the data guide your focus.
